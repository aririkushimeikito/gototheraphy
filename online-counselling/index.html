Online counselling

The same checks, from your own room.

Online counselling is therapy by video call or phone with a qualified therapist, instead of in a room together. The therapists listed here for online work have had their registration, insurance and supervision checked by a person, exactly as for in-person work. It suits people who cannot travel, have unpredictable hours, or find it easier to talk from home.

Therapists who work online

Can I have therapy online?

Yes. Most of the therapists listed here offer online sessions, by video or by phone, and many work that way entirely. You choose a therapist in the same way, read the same four questions, and see the same list of what was checked. The only practical difference is where you sit.

Who does online counselling suit?

People who live somewhere with few therapists, or where the one they want to see is an hour away. People with caring responsibilities or shift patterns that make a fixed weekly journey impossible. People who do not want to be seen walking into a building. People who find it easier to say difficult things when they are not in the same room. None of these is a lesser reason.

What changes when the room is a screen?

You have to make the room. A door that closes, headphones if you share a wall, and a slot when you will not be interrupted. Some people sit in the car. The therapist will usually check at the start that you are somewhere private.

The ending is more abrupt. In person, there is a corridor and a street. Online, the call ends and you are back in your kitchen. Many people find it helps to keep ten minutes clear afterwards.

Some things are harder to read on a screen, and a good therapist will ask more often whether something has landed. You are allowed to say “I would rather do this in person” if you find that out after a few sessions.

Setting it up so it works

  • Ask what they use

    Most therapists use a mainstream video service. Ask before the first session, and test it once so the first ten minutes are not spent on the software.

  • Headphones, if you can

    They keep the therapist's voice private and make it easier to hear the quiet parts.

  • Keep the edges clear

    Five minutes before, ten after. The session is the same length as in person; the transitions are yours to make.

  • Check the same things

    Online therapists are checked identically: register, insurance, supervision, identity. If a website offers therapy online with no register named, that is worth noticing.
