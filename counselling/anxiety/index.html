About anxiety

What anxiety tends to feel like day to day

Anxiety is rarely one big moment. More often it is a low hum that runs underneath everything: the email you re-read four times, the phone call you put off until it becomes a problem, the Sunday evening that starts at lunchtime. Your body may be tight in a way you stop noticing. Sleep may be shallow. You may be very good at hiding it, which is its own kind of tiring.

People describe it differently. Some talk about dread, some about restlessness, some about a mind that will not stop planning for things that never happen. There is no right way to have it.

When it is worth talking to somebody

You do not need to reach a threshold. If anxiety is shaping your decisions, costing you sleep, or making you smaller than you want to be, that is enough reason to talk to a therapist. You are allowed to seek help for something that other people would call manageable. You are also allowed to not come back after a first session if it is not right.

What kinds of therapy people work with for anxiety

Several approaches are commonly used, and the therapists listed here describe theirs in their own words. Cognitive behavioural therapy works with the link between thoughts, feelings and what you do next. Person-centred and humanistic approaches give you room to say what is happening without being steered. Psychodynamic work looks at where patterns come from. Many therapists describe themselves as integrative, meaning they draw on more than one approach depending on the person in front of them.

None of these is ranked here. Which one suits you depends on how you like to work and who you can talk to. A therapist's profile should explain their approach plainly. If it does not, ask.

What a first session about anxiety tends to cover

A first session is usually a conversation about what has been happening, how long it has been going on, and what you would like to be different. The therapist may ask what you have already tried. You can ask how they work and what a typical run of sessions looks like. Nothing needs to be decided in the room. Most therapists expect you to go away and think about whether it felt right.
