Looking for a therapist in Brighton

Looking for a therapist in Brighton is easier than in most towns of its size, and harder than it looks. There are a lot of practitioners, spread between the city centre, Hove, Kemptown and the villages along the Downs. Parking near the seafront is expensive and slow. Most people choose somewhere on a bus route or within a short walk of Brighton or Hove station, and many therapists offer early morning or evening hours because so many clients commute to London.

Brighton has two universities and a large student population, which means there are practitioners who work a great deal with people in their late teens and twenties. It also has a long-established LGBTQ+ community, and a number of therapists say plainly on their profiles that this is where much of their work sits. The creative and digital industries are a large local employer, and freelancers and people between contracts make up a noticeable part of many caseloads.

Online sessions are common here. Some people choose them because a lunchtime slot from a shared flat or a co-working space is easier than a journey across the city. Others start online with a therapist in Brighton and move to the room later, or keep working online when they leave for a job elsewhere. If the therapist you want to see is in Hove and you are in Saltdean, online may be the difference between going and not going.
