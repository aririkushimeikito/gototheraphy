How it works

Finding a therapist, without the part where you guess.

To find a therapist in the UK: search a directory where the therapist's registration has been checked, read a few profiles, contact one or two directly, and treat the first session as a conversation rather than a commitment. Ask about cost in your first message. If it is not right, you are allowed to not go back and try somebody else.

  1. 01

    Search

    By town, by what you want help with, or online only. Filters narrow the list. Nothing ranks it, and there are no reviews.

  2. 02

    Read two or three profiles

    Each therapist answers four questions in their own words, explains how they work in plain English, and lists what was checked.

  3. 03

    Get in touch directly

    You write to or call the therapist. Say what you are looking for in a sentence, and ask about cost and availability. Many offer a short call first.

  4. 04

    Have a first session, then decide

    The first session is for finding out whether you can talk to this person. You do not have to decide anything in the room.

What does a first therapy session involve?

A first session is usually a conversation about what has brought you, how long it has been going on, and what you would like to be different. The therapist may ask what you have already tried and whether anything is urgent. You can ask how they work, how often they would expect to see you, and what happens if you want to stop.

It can feel awkward. Most people find the first ten minutes the hardest. You do not have to tell the whole story, and you do not have to be articulate about it. “I do not know where to start” is a normal first sentence.

Nothing needs to be decided in the room. Most therapists expect you to go away and think about whether it felt right, and will say so.

How much does therapy cost?

Private therapists set their own fees, and the fee is printed on the profile when the therapist has entered it. Where it is not, ask in your first message. You are allowed to ask about cost before anything else, and a therapist will not think less of you for it. Some offer a smaller number of reduced-fee places; the profile says so where that is the case.

Gototherapy does not type a typical fee or a range anywhere on the site. Where a range appears in future, it is computed from what members have entered, on the day you look.

If you cannot pay, you should not leave with nothing. In England you can refer yourself to NHS Talking Therapies without seeing a GP, and your GP can also refer you. Waiting times vary by area. Elsewhere in the UK, your GP is the usual route.

How do I know if it is the right fit?

After two or three sessions you should be able to answer three questions. Do you feel listened to. Do you feel able to say when something does not land. Does the therapist explain what they are doing when you ask. If the answer to any of these is no, say so in the session. If it does not change, it is reasonable to try somebody else. Changing therapist is not a failure and does not need a justification.

More questions people ask

What is the difference between a counsellor and a psychotherapist?

In the UK the titles overlap and neither is protected by law, which is why the register matters more than the word. Both are checked here against their professional register. A profile explains the therapist's approach in plain English, and you can ask them how they describe the difference.

Can I have therapy online?

Yes. Many therapists listed here work by video or phone, with identical verification. What changes when the room is a screen.

What does verified mean here?

A person has checked the therapist's register entry, seen a current insurance certificate, confirmed their supervision with the supervisor, and matched their identity to the register. It is repeated every year and it differs by role. How we verify.
